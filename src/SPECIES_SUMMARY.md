# Expanded Coral Guide - 18 USVI Species

## Overview
Expanded from 5 to **18 coral species** commonly found or historically important in USVI waters. Species selected based on:
- Recent NOAA monitoring data (2023-2025)
- Historical reef composition studies
- Conservation importance
- USVI restoration priorities
- Visual distinctiveness for ID

## Species List by Conservation Status

### Critically Endangered (3 species)
1. **Elkhorn Coral** (*Acropora palmata*) - Historically dominant shallow reef species
2. **Staghorn Coral** (*Acropora cervicornis*) - Key restoration species
3. **Pillar Coral** (*Dendrogyra cylindrus*) - Very rare, unique growth form

### Endangered (3 species)
4. **Mountainous Star Coral** (*Orbicella faveolata*) - Formerly most abundant on USVI reefs
5. **Lobed Star Coral** (*Orbicella annularis*) - Important reef builder
6. **Boulder Star Coral** (*Orbicella franksi*) - Part of Orbicella complex

### Vulnerable (5 species)
7. **Boulder Brain Coral** (*Colpophyllia natans*) - Large, long-lived species
8. **Symmetrical Brain Coral** (*Pseudodiploria strigosa*) - Common brain coral
9. **Grooved Brain Coral** (*Diploria labyrinthiformis*) - Distinctive grooved pattern
10. **Smooth Flower Coral** (*Eusmilia fastigiata*) - Less common, distinctive
11. **Elliptical Star Coral** (*Dichocoenia stokesii*) - Small, distinctive corallites

### Least Concern (7 species)
12. **Mustard Hill Coral** (*Porites astreoides*) - MOST COMMON on modern USVI reefs
13. **Lettuce Coral** (*Agaricia agaricites*) - Second most common species
14. **Massive Starlet Coral** (*Siderastrea siderea*) - Third most common, very hardy
15. **Great Star Coral** (*Montastraea cavernosa*) - Large, distinctive polyps
16. **Finger Coral** (*Porites porites*) - Common in bays and protected areas
17. **Lesser Starlet Coral** (*Siderastrea radians*) - Very hardy, small colonies
18. **Blushing Star Coral** (*Stephanocoenia intersepta*) - Often pinkish coloration

## Key Insights from USVI Research

### Current Reef Composition (2023-2025)
According to recent NOAA NCRMP surveys:
- **Most common species**: *Porites astreoides*, *Agaricia agaricites*, *Siderastrea siderea*
- These are **fast-growing, stress-tolerant** species
- They **contribute less to reef-building** than historical dominants
- Shift toward **smaller colonies**, especially in disease-susceptible species

### Historical vs. Current
**Before 1980s:**
- Dominated by *Acropora palmata* (Elkhorn) in shallow water
- *Orbicella* complex (Mountainous/Lobed/Boulder Star) on reef slopes
- Large, complex 3D reef structures

**Current Status:**
- Massive declines in Acropora (>90% loss)
- Orbicella complex severely impacted by disease
- Shift to hardier, weedy species
- Loss of structural complexity

### Major Threats
1. **Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease (SCTLD)** - Primary current threat
2. **White Band Disease** - Devastated Acropora species
3. **Bleaching events** - Increasing frequency
4. **Hurricanes** - Major physical damage
5. **Climate change** - Ocean warming and acidification

## Species Organization by Growth Form

### Branching Corals
- Elkhorn Coral (thick, flat branches)
- Staghorn Coral (thin, cylindrical branches)
- Finger Coral (thick finger-like branches)
- Pillar Coral (tall columns - unique)

### Massive/Boulder Corals
- Mountainous Star Coral (tall bumps/columns)
- Lobed Star Coral (columns/pillars)
- Boulder Star Coral (massive boulder)
- Great Star Coral (large polyps, massive)
- Massive Starlet Coral (small polyps, dome)

### Brain Corals (Meandering Valleys)
- Boulder Brain Coral (wide valleys)
- Symmetrical Brain Coral (narrow, regular valleys)
- Grooved Brain Coral (deep grooves)

### Plating Corals
- Lettuce Coral (thin plates/leaves)

### Small/Encrusting Corals
- Mustard Hill Coral (domes/encrusting)
- Lesser Starlet Coral (small domes)
- Elliptical Star Coral (small mounds)
- Blushing Star Coral (encrusting to mounds)
- Smooth Flower Coral (small branching)
